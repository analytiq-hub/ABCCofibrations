# Over and under categories (§1.7)

The implementation is [OverUnder.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/OverUnder.lean)
(statement (1)) and
[OverUnderFibration.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/OverUnderFibration.lean)
(statement (2)), with the transport lemma in
[EquivalenceTransfer.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/EquivalenceTransfer.lean), and
the CF5–CF6 parts in
[OverUnderCofibrationCategory.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/OverUnderCofibrationCategory.lean).
The source is §1.7 of
[*Cofibrations in Homotopy Theory*, v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610009v4)
(`references/radulescu-banu-2009/source/chap-cofcat.tex`).

## Differences from the paper

* Proposition 1.7.1 is formalized in full, both for precofibration
  (CF1–CF4) and for cofibration (CF1–CF6) categories, and dually. Fibration
  categories (F1–F6) are defined as cofibration categories on the opposite
  category (`ABCFibrationCategory`, in
  [FibrationCategory.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/FibrationCategory.lean)).
* The over and under categories are mathlib's `Over A` and `Under A`, i.e.
  $(\mathcal M\downarrow A)$ and $(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ for the identity
  functor. The induced classes on `Over A` are mathlib's own
  (`Mathlib.AlgebraicTopology.ModelCategory.Over`). Mathlib lists those on
  `Under A` as a TODO. Here they are generated from mathlib's `Over`
  instances by mathlib's `to_dual` attribute: the dual of the fibrations of
  `Over A` are the cofibrations of `Under A`, and weak equivalences are
  self-dual. Mathlib does not yet register the needed dual pairs, so
  [ToDualRegistrations.lean](../../ABCCofibrations/ToDualRegistrations.lean)
  registers them locally; it is to be removed once mathlib registers them.
  The characterizations `cofibration_under_iff` and `weakEquivalence_under_iff`
  are stated directly, because `to_dual` cannot yet translate proofs that
  pass a morphism property through `Iff`/`simp` rewrites.
* The paper calls the result "a simple consequence of the definitions". The
  formal proof makes explicit the two facts it relies on: cofibrancy in the
  over and under categories, and the computation of pushouts in $\mathcal M$.

## Statement

*Let $\mathcal M$ be a precofibration category and $A$ an object. With the
induced weak equivalences and cofibrations,*

*(a) $(\mathcal M\downarrow A)$ is a precofibration category;*

*(b) if $A$ is cofibrant, $(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ is a precofibration
category.*

## Proof outline

**Cofibrant objects.** The initial object of $(\mathcal M\downarrow A)$ is
$\varnothing\to A$, whose underlying object is initial in $\mathcal M$; so an
object $X\to A$ is cofibrant exactly when $X$ is
(`isCofibrant_over_iff`, using that cofibrancy does not depend on the chosen
initial object). The initial object of $(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ is
$1_A$, so an object $A\xrightarrow{x}X$ is cofibrant exactly when $x$ is a
cofibration (`isCofibrant_under_iff`). This is where $A$ must be cofibrant:
CF1 asks for the initial object $1_A$ to be cofibrant. Then $X$ is cofibrant.

**Pushouts.** Both forgetful functors create pushouts: for `Under A` because
spans are connected, for `Over A` because the forgetful functor creates all
colimits that exist in $\mathcal M$. So the pushouts required by CF3 exist,
and a pushout square upstairs is a pushout square of underlying objects;
CF3 in $\mathcal M$ then gives CF3 upstairs.

**Factorization.** For $f:X\to Y$ with $X$ cofibrant, factor the underlying
map in $\mathcal M$ as $X\xrightarrow{i}Z\xrightarrow{p}Y$ and give $Z$ the
structure map $Z\to Y\to A$ (over $A$), resp. $A\to X\to Z$ (under $A$).

CF1's statements about isomorphisms and CF2 hold because isomorphisms and
two out of three are detected on underlying maps.

## Statement (2): prefibration categories

*Let $\mathcal M$ be a prefibration category and $A$ an object. With the
induced weak equivalences and fibrations, (a) $(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ is a
prefibration category; (b) if $A$ is fibrant, $(\mathcal M\downarrow A)$ is a
prefibration category.*

A prefibration structure on $\mathcal D$ is a precofibration structure on
$\mathcal D^{op}$. Mathlib's equivalences
$(A\downarrow\mathcal M)^{op}\simeq(\mathcal M^{op}\downarrow A)$ and
$(\mathcal M\downarrow A)^{op}\simeq(A\downarrow\mathcal M^{op})$
(`Over.opEquivOpUnder`, `Under.opEquivOpOver`) identify the induced classes
on both sides, definitionally. A precofibration structure transfers along any
equivalence of categories identifying the cofibrations and weak equivalences
(`ABCPrecofibrationCategory.of_equivalence`: cofibrancy, pushouts and
factorizations are carried across by the equivalence and its unit and
counit). So (2)(a) is (1)(a) for $\mathcal M^{op}$, and (2)(b) is (1)(b) for
$\mathcal M^{op}$, using that $A$ is cofibrant in $\mathcal M^{op}$ when it is
fibrant in $\mathcal M$. The fibrations of `Under A` are generated by
`to_dual` from mathlib's cofibrations of `Over A`, like the cofibrations.

## CF5 and CF6

**Transfer along a forgetful functor.** Let $U:\mathcal D\to\mathcal M$ detect
cofibrations and weak equivalences. If $U$ creates sequential colimits and
preserves cofibrancy, CF6 transfers from $\mathcal M$ to $\mathcal D$: the
colimit of a CF6 sequence upstairs exists, and $U$ sends its first colimit
leg to the colimit leg downstairs up to an isomorphism. If $U$ creates small
coproducts and detects cofibrancy, CF5 transfers likewise, using the
comparison isomorphism $\coprod UX_i\cong U\coprod X_i$.

**Over categories.** `Over.forget` creates all colimits and detects
cofibrancy, so $(\mathcal M\downarrow A)$ is a cofibration category.

**Under categories.** Sequential colimits are connected, so `Under.forget`
creates them, and CF6 transfers. Coproducts in $(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ are
not computed in $\mathcal M$: the coproduct of $A\to X_i$ is the pushout

$$
\textstyle\coprod_A X_i=A\amalg_{\coprod A}\coprod X_i
$$

along the fold map $\coprod A\to A$. For cofibrant $X_i$ (structure maps
cofibrations) the coproducts exist by CF5 and the pushout by CF3, and the
structure map $A\to\coprod_A X_i$ is a cobase change of the cofibration
$\coprod A\to\coprod X_i$. For maps $f_i:X_i\to Y_i$, pasting shows that
$\coprod_A X_i\to\coprod_A Y_i$ is a cobase change of the ambient sum
$\coprod X_i\to\coprod Y_i$ along $\coprod X_i\to\coprod_A X_i$, so CF5 for
$(A\downarrow\mathcal M)$ follows from CF5 and CF3 in $\mathcal M$.

**Fibration categories.** A cofibration structure also transfers along an
equivalence identifying the classes (`ABCCofibrationCategory.of_equivalence`),
since an equivalence creates all colimits. Statement (2) for fibration
categories then follows from (1) by the op-equivalences, as for F1–F4.
